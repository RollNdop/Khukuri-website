The craft

How a khukuri is made

Four stages, the same way they've always been done in this workshop — no factories, no shortcuts.

1

Heat

Steel goes into the forge fire until it glows and moves like it wants to be shaped. Reading that glow correctly — not too soon, not too late — is the first thing any apprentice has to learn.

2

Hammer

Every curve comes from a hammer, not a mold. One strike at a time, the belly, the spine, and the notch near the handle take shape under a smith who's done this thousands of times.

3

Quench & temper

Plunged into water to harden fast, then carefully reheated to temper it back. Skip either step and the blade is either too brittle to trust or too soft to hold an edge — this is where a khukuri actually becomes one.

4

Fit & finish

Last comes the part most people never think about: fitting a handle, bolster, and hand-carved sheath to a blade that doesn't come in standard sizes. Nothing leaves this workshop unchecked.

Materials

Like most traditional Nepali workshops, khukuris are typically forged from high-carbon steel, with handles shaped from hardwood, buffalo horn, or brass, and sheaths built from a wood core wrapped in leather.
