The craft

How a khukuri is made

Four stages, the same way they've always been done in this workshop — no factories, no shortcuts.

1

Heat

Steel is brought to the forge fire until it glows and is soft enough to move. Getting the temperature right, by eye and experience, is the first skill any apprentice learns.

2

Hammer

The blade is shaped entirely by hand, one strike at a time, into the recurved khukuri profile — the belly, the spine, and the notch near the handle all take shape here.

3

Quench & temper

The blade is cooled rapidly to harden the edge, then carefully reheated to temper it — the step that gives a khukuri its balance of hardness and flex.

4

Fit & finish

The handle, bolster, and sheath are shaped and fitted by hand, and the edge is finished by hand before the blade ever leaves the workshop.

Materials

Like most traditional Nepali workshops, khukuris are typically forged from high-carbon steel, with handles shaped from hardwood, buffalo horn, or brass, and sheaths built from a wood core wrapped in leather.
